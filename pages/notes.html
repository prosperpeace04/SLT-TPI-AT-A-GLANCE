ℹ️STC 211 — Inorganic Chemistry II (ND2 First Semester). Topics: Group II Elements and Water, Hard and Soft Water, Group III Elements, Group IV Elements.

Effect of Water on Group II Elements

The Group II elements are Beryllium (Be), Magnesium (Mg), Calcium (Ca), Strontium (Sr), Barium (Ba) and Radium (Ra). They are called alkaline earth metals and react vigorously with cold water except Beryllium which does not react with water.

In each reaction, hydrogen gas is given off and the metal hydroxide is produced. Calcium and magnesium dissolved in water are the two most common minerals that make water hard.

Reactions of Mg and Ca with Water

  • Magnesium: No reaction with cold water. Reacts with steam to form MgO and hydrogen gas.
Mg(s) + H₂O(g) → MgO + H₂
  • Calcium: Reacts with cold water to produce calcium hydroxide and hydrogen gas.
Ca(s) + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂

Hard Water and Soft Water

Definitions

  • Soft Water: Gives ready and permanent lather with soap. Examples: rain water, distilled water.
  • Hard Water: Does not give ready and permanent lather with soap. Examples: sea water, river water, tap water.

Types of Hardness

  • Temporary Hardness: Due to dissolved hydrogen carbonates. Removed by boiling.
  • Permanent Hardness: Due to calcium/magnesium sulfates, chlorides and nitrates. Cannot be removed by boiling.

Removal of Temporary Hardness

Method 1: Boiling

Ca(HCO₃)₂ → CaCO₃ + CO₂ + H₂O Mg(HCO₃)₂ → MgCO₃ + CO₂ + H₂O

Method 2: Clark's Process

Ca(HCO₃)₂ + Ca(OH)₂ → 2CaCO₃ + 2H₂O Mg(HCO₃)₂ + Ca(OH)₂ → 2CaCO₃ + Mg(OH)₂ + 2H₂O

Removal of Permanent Hardness

Method 1: Adding Washing Soda (Na₂CO₃)

CaCl₂ + Na₂CO₃ → CaCO₃ + 2NaCl MgSO₄ + Na₂CO₃ → MgCO₃ + Na₂SO₄

Method 2: Ion Exchange (Permutit/Zeolite)

Na₂Z + Ca²⁺ → CaZ + 2Na⁺ Na₂Z + Mg²⁺ → MgZ + 2Na⁺

EDTA Titration for Hardness Estimation

Ca²⁺(aq) + H₂Y²⁻(aq) → CaY²⁻(aq) + 2H⁺(aq)

Eriochrome Black T indicator at pH 10 — endpoint: wine red → blue.

Advantages and Disadvantages of Hard Water

Advantages

  • Helps animals build shells (calcium carbonate).
  • Tastes better due to dissolved minerals.
  • Builds strong teeth and bones.
  • Does not dissolve lead pipes.

Disadvantages

  • Requires a lot of soap to form lather.
  • Causes furring of kettles and boilers.
  • Not advisable for washing white fabrics.
  • Cannot be used in dyeing and tanning.

Group III Elements (Boron Group)

Members: Boron (B), Aluminium (Al), Gallium (Ga), Indium (In), Thallium (Tl). All have 3 valence electrons; primary oxidation state +3.

Anomalous Behaviour of Boron

  • It is a non-metal while other members are metallic.
  • Forms only covalent compounds.
  • Oxides and hydroxides are weakly acidic; Al is amphoteric.

Group IV Elements (Carbon Group)

Members: Carbon (C), Silicon (Si), Germanium (Ge), Tin (Sn), Lead (Pb). Valence shell: ns²np².

C and Si are non-metals, Ge is a metalloid, Sn and Pb are metals. Exhibit +2 and +4 oxidation states.

ℹ️STC 212 — Instrumental Analytical Chemistry and Quality Control (ND2). Topics: Spectrophotometry, Beer-Lambert Law, AAS, AES, Flame Photometry.

Spectrophotometry

A technique that uses light to measure chemical concentrations that a sample absorbs. The instrument is a Spectrophotometer containing a prism or grating monochromator.

Key Relationships

c = λv where: c = speed of light (3.0 × 10⁸ m/s), λ = wavelength, v = frequency E = hv where: h = Planck's constant = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ Js

Beer-Lambert's Law

The absorbance of a solution is directly proportional to the concentration of the absorbing species at a constant path length.

A = εcl where: A = Absorbance (no unit) ε = molar absorptivity (L/mol/cm) c = concentration (mol/L) l = path length (cm)

AAS Instrumentation — Five Components

  • Light source emitting the spectrum of the element of interest.
  • Monochromator for light dispersion.
  • Absorption cell where atoms of the sample are produced.
  • Detector measuring light intensity.
  • Read-out device displaying the reading.

Single-Beam vs Double-Beam

Single-Beam: Suited for single wavelength absorbance; simple and easy to maintain.

Double-Beam: Light divided into two beams (sample + reference). Greater speed and convenience; no lamp warm-up needed.

Atomic Emission Spectroscopy (AES)

Similar to AAS but no light source needed. Excited atoms emit characteristic radiation as they return to ground state. Emission intensity at a characteristic wavelength is nearly proportional to concentration.

Flame Photometry

Used mainly for alkali metals analysis, particularly in biological fluids. Uses an air-butane or propane flame. Spectral selection device: optical filters. Can analyse Na, K, Ca.

Mode of Operation

  • Open gases and mix in right proportion to obtain a blue flame.
  • Aspirate distilled/deionised water.
  • Adjust galvanometer reading to zero.
  • Aspirate solutions of standards and analyte.
ℹ️STP 211 — Electronics (ND2 First Semester). Lecturer: Mr. Moses N. James. Topics: Semiconductors, P-N Junction, Diodes, Transistors, Thermionic Valves.

Electronic Structure of Elements

Electrons revolve only in permitted orbits identified using letters K, L, M, N, P etc. The shells are known by their principal quantum number n.

Maximum electrons per shell = 2n² K shell (n=1): 2 electrons L shell (n=2): 8 electrons M shell (n=3): 18 electrons

Energy Bands

Valence Band: Range of energies possessed by valence electrons. May be completely or partially filled.

Conduction Band: Range of energies possessed by conduction electrons. Insulators have empty conduction bands.

Forbidden Energy Gap: Separation between conduction and valence bands. Greater gap = more tightly bound electrons.

Conductors, Semiconductors and Insulators

Conductors: Valence and conduction bands overlap. No forbidden energy gap.

Semiconductors: Small forbidden energy gap (~1 eV). Valence band almost filled, conduction band almost empty.

Insulators: Very large energy gap (~15 eV). Valence band full, conduction band empty.

Intrinsic and Extrinsic Semiconductors

Intrinsic: Extremely pure semiconductor (Si or Ge).

Extrinsic: Semiconductor doped with impurities to increase conductivity.

DOPING = adding impurities to increase and control conductivity n-type: pentavalent impurity added → more free electrons p-type: trivalent impurity added → more holes

P-N Junction Diode

When the junction is formed, a Depletion/Barrier Layer forms — a region free of majority charge carriers.

Forward Bias: Positive to p-type, negative to n-type. Cancels potential barrier → current flows.

Reverse Bias: Negative to p-type, positive to n-type. Increases potential barrier → current blocked.

Breakdown Mechanisms

Avalanche Breakdown: Carriers gain enough energy to cause an avalanche — massive increase in reverse current.

Zener Breakdown: Heavily doped junction, very narrow depletion layer, intense field breaks covalent bonds directly.

Types of Diodes and Applications

  • Zener Diode: Voltage regulators, peak clippers, calibrating voltmeters.
  • Tunnel Diode: Microwave oscillator (~10 GHz), logic memory, ultrahigh-speed switch.
  • LED: Indicator lamps, seven-segment displays, numeric displays.
  • Photodiode: Fast counters, encoders, optical communication, demodulation.
  • Varactor Diode: AFC device, FM modulator, adjustable band-pass filter.

Thermionic Valves

Triode: Cathode, anode, control grid. Small change in grid voltage produces large change in anode current.

Tetrode: Adds screen grid. Provides electrostatic shielding between anode and control grid. Anode current nearly constant over large anode voltage variation.

Pentode: Adds suppressor grid. Prevents secondary electrons from reaching screen grid. Increases amplification factor.

ℹ️Control of Microorganisms. Topics: Methods of control, sterilization, disinfection, physical and chemical agents.

Definition of Terms

  • Sterilization: Destroys all forms of life. Objects are free from all living cells, spores and viruses.
  • Disinfection: Kills vegetative cells but not spores. Applied to inanimate objects.
  • Antiseptic: Prevents sepsis; can be applied to body tissues without injury.
  • Sanitizer: Reduces microbial population to safe levels. Applied to inanimate objects in food industry.
  • Germicide: Kills vegetative cells but not spores.
  • Bacteriocide: Kills bacterial cells but not spores.
  • Bacteriostatic: Stops the growth of bacteria.
  • Preservative: Prevents growth of microorganisms.

Moist Heat Methods

  • Boiling (100°C, 10 min): Kills vegetative bacteria and many viruses. NOT reliable sterilization — endospores survive.
  • Tyndallization (Fractional Sterilization): 100°C for 20 min for three consecutive days with 24-hour incubation between. Invented by John Tyndall.
  • Pasteurization: HTST = 71.6°C for 15 seconds. LTH = 62.9°C for 30 minutes. UHT = 140°C for 1–2 seconds.
  • Autoclaving: Steam under pressure at ~15 psi (121°C) for 15 minutes. Kills all organisms and endospores.

Dry Heat and Other Physical Methods

  • Incineration: Direct flaming. 100% effective.
  • Hot Air Oven: 160°C for 1 hour. For glassware, forceps, scissors, swabs.
  • Freezing: Bacteriostatic — stops metabolic processes. Rarely kills bacteria.
  • UV Light: 260 nm wavelength damages nucleic acids. Poor penetrating power — surface sterilization only.
  • Ionising Radiation (Gamma rays from Co-60): High penetrating power. Used for surgical supplies, food preservation.
  • Filtration: Membrane filters (0.22 μm) remove bacteria. HEPA filters create clean air in operating theatres.

Chemical Methods of Control

  • Phenol/Phenolics: Denatures proteins, destabilizes membranes. Lysol = cresol + soap.
  • Iodine: Effective against bacteria, endospores, fungi, some viruses. Available as Tincture or Betadine.
  • Chlorine: Acts via hypochlorous acid → nascent oxygen → oxidizes cellular enzymes.
  • Alcohols (70–80%): Denatures proteins and dissolves lipids. Isopropanol has highest germicidal power.
  • Quaternary Ammonium Compounds (Quats): Highly bactericidal against gram+ve. Alter membrane permeability.
  • Heavy Metals (Oligodynamic Action): Combine with –SH groups of enzymes, denaturing them. Silver, Mercury, Copper.
  • Ethylene Oxide: Alkylating agent; deep penetration; can sterilize packaged materials.
  • Chlorhexidine: Broad-spectrum cationic antiseptic. Binds to negatively charged surfaces in oral cavity.
  • Glutaraldehyde: Disinfects medical/dental equipment. 10 min for disinfection, 12 hours for spores.
ℹ️Introduction to Microsoft Excel for SLT. Topics: Spreadsheet basics, Excel interface, workbook structure, functions (SUM, MIN, MAX, AVERAGE, COUNT).

Introduction to Microsoft Excel

Microsoft Excel is a spreadsheet software developed by Microsoft — part of the Microsoft 365 suite. It organizes, analyzes, stores and calculates data in a tabular format using a grid of rows, columns and cells.

Other spreadsheet packages: Lotus 1-2-3, Quattro, Multiplan, Framework, VisiCalc.

Excel Ribbon Tabs

  • Home: Clipboard, formatting, insert/delete rows, editing commands.
  • Insert: Insert tables, diagrams, charts, symbols.
  • Page Layout: Overall worksheet appearance and printing.
  • Formulas: Insert formulas, name cells, formula auditing.
  • Data: Data-related commands and validation.
  • Review: Spell check, translate, comments, protect sheets.
  • View: Control how a sheet is viewed.

Workbook Structure

  • Workbook: Excel file with at least one worksheet.
  • Worksheet: Working surface divided into rows and columns.
  • Rows: Horizontal lines identified by numbers (1, 2, 3...).
  • Columns: Vertical lines identified by letters (A, B, C...).
  • Cell: Intersection of row and column (e.g. A1). Each has a Cell Address.
  • Active Cell: The selected cell where data is entered.
  • Range: Selected set of multiple cells.

Key Excel Functions

=SUM(C2:E2) — adds values in range =MIN(C2:E2) — returns smallest value =MAX(C2:E2) — returns largest value =AVERAGE(C2:E2) — returns average of range =COUNT(C2:E2) — counts cells with numbers

ℹ️Research Methodology and Basic Statistics. Topics: Types of research, research design, sampling, central tendency, dispersion.

Types of Research

  • Descriptive vs Analytical: Descriptive reports what is; analytical evaluates available data critically.
  • Applied vs Fundamental: Applied solves immediate practical problems; fundamental builds theory.
  • Quantitative vs Qualitative: Quantitative measures amount; qualitative investigates reasons and meanings.
  • Conceptual vs Empirical: Conceptual deals with abstract ideas; empirical relies on observation/experiment.

Measures of Central Tendency

Mean (x̄) = Σx / n Median = middle value when arranged in order Mode = value that appears most frequently

Example: Mean of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 = 30/5 = 6.

Bimodal: two modes. Trimodal: three modes. No mode: no value appears more than once.

Measures of Dispersion

  • Variation: Measure of spread of data.
  • Standard Deviation: Measure of dispersion from the mean.
  • Variance: Square of standard deviation.

Types of Statistical Tests

  • Summary statistics (mean, standard deviation).
  • Two-sample differences tests (t-test).
  • Non-parametric tests (U-test).
  • Matched pairs tests (Wilcoxon).
  • Association tests (Chi-squared).

Representation of Data

  • Bar Graph: Rectangular bars with lengths proportional to values.
  • Pie Chart: Circle divided into sectors representing proportions.
  • Line Graph: Series of data points connected with straight lines.
  • Histogram: Rectangles with area proportional to frequency; width = class interval.
  • Pictograph: Data shown using pictures/symbols.
  • Frequency Distribution: Data values arranged in order with corresponding frequencies.
ℹ️STB 211 — Pest and Pest Control (ND2). Topics: Resistance varieties, biological control, cultural methods, IPM, pesticides.

Resistance Varieties of Crops

Types of Resistance

  • Natural Resistance: Evolved biochemical or physical traits (toxins, thick cuticles, hairs).
  • Biotechnological (GM Crops): Bt cotton/corn produce proteins toxic to specific pests.
  • Gene Stacking: Multiple resistance genes combined — pests must overcome several defenses simultaneously.

Mechanisms

  • Antixenosis: Crop less attractive — deters feeding/egg-laying.
  • Tolerance: Crop endures damage without significant yield loss.
  • Insecticidal Properties: Natural toxins or GM compounds toxic to pests.

Biological Techniques in Pest Control

  • Predators: Ladybugs (aphids), lacewing larvae, predatory beetles, birds, bats.
  • Parasitoids: Trichogramma wasps (parasitize moth eggs), Tachinid flies (parasitize caterpillars).
  • Pathogens: Bt bacteria (caterpillars), Beauveria bassiana fungus (aphids, whiteflies), NPV virus (caterpillars), Steinernema nematodes (soil pests).
  • Competitive Exclusion: Trichoderma fungi outcompete root disease pathogens.

Cultural Methods in Pest Control

  • Crop Rotation: Breaks pest lifecycle. E.g. cereals with legumes.
  • Intercropping: Two+ crops together disrupt pest preferences.
  • Trap Cropping: Attractive crop lures pests away from main crop.
  • Sanitation: Remove plant debris, fallen fruits, weeds.
  • Soil Solarization: Clear plastic traps solar energy, kills soil pests and pathogens.
  • Mulching: Prevents weed growth and soil-borne pest access.

Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

Combines multiple methods — monitoring, prevention, biological, mechanical, and chemical control — to minimize pest impact while minimizing environmental harm.

Key Components

  • Monitoring and Identification
  • Prevention (crop rotation, resistant varieties, physical barriers)
  • Biological Control (natural predators, pathogens)
  • Mechanical Control (hand-picking, traps, tillage)
  • Chemical Control (selective pesticides only when necessary)

Pesticide Classifications

By Target Species

  • Insecticides, Herbicides, Fungicides, Rodenticides, Nematicides, Acaricides (miticides), Molluscicides, Bactericides, Algicides.

By Chemical Origin

  • Natural Organic: Pyrethrins, Neem oil, Spinosad, Bt.
  • Synthetic Organic: Organophosphates (Malathion), Carbamates (Carbaryl), Pyrethroids (Permethrin).
  • Inorganic: Sulfur, Copper compounds, Boric acid, Diatomaceous earth.

Precautions for Safe Use

  • Always wear PPE (gloves, goggles, mask).
  • Read and follow label instructions.
  • Apply during calm weather; avoid near water sources.
  • Triple-rinse empty containers before disposal.
